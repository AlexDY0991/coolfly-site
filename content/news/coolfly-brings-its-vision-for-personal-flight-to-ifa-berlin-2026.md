**BERLIN, Germany, September 3, 2026 — CoolFly will showcase its Dream ST personal electric aircraft at IFA Berlin 2026, taking place September 4–8 at Messe Berlin. Visitors can see Dream ST at CityCube Hall B, Booth CCBB-143, in IFA’s Mobility area.**

![IFA exhibition](/assets/img/news/coolfly-brings-its-vision-for-personal-flight-to-ifa-berlin-2026-1.jpg)

Following its appearance at EAA AirVenture Oshkosh in the United States earlier this year and the global launch of its latest product lineup in Hangzhou in August, CoolFly is bringing personal aviation to one of the world’s leading consumer technology stages.

At IFA, Dream ST will be the CoolFly aircraft on physical display, giving visitors, media and potential partners a close-up look at the company’s approach to compact, electric personal flight.

For CoolFly, however, the aircraft itself is only part of the story. Behind Dream ST is a broader product portfolio and a self-developed flight-control platform designed to serve as the technological foundation across that portfolio.

## From One Aircraft to a Personal Flight Portfolio

CoolFly’s current lineup consists of **Dream ST, Dream Pro and Urban**, covering different approaches to personal electric flight.

The aircraft appearing at IFA, **Dream ST**, is a lightweight, single-seat electric aircraft built around personal and recreational flying. Its seated configuration and quick-fold design are intended to make transportation and storage more practical for individual owners, while its development is oriented toward ultralight aircraft requirements in markets including the United States.

![Visitor seat in the CoolFly DREAM aircraft](/assets/img/news/coolfly-brings-its-vision-for-personal-flight-to-ifa-berlin-2026-2.jpg)

For users seeking greater capability, **Dream Pro** extends the Dream platform with increased payload, endurance and intelligent flight features. It supports a maximum payload of **120 kg**, has a maximum flight speed of **100 km/h**, and has achieved **40 minutes of flight time in manned flight testing with a 70 kg pilot onboard**. It also incorporates technologies including millimeter-wave obstacle detection, intelligent route planning and obstacle avoidance, together with safety features such as a whole-aircraft parachute system and impact-mitigation airbags.

At the center of CoolFly’s product portfolio is its flagship **[Urban](https://coolflyaircraft.com/coolfly-evtol-mass-production-and-public-flights-in-2026/)**, a standing-position personal aircraft featuring an enclosed-propeller architecture. The standing configuration creates a fundamentally different relationship between the pilot and the aircraft, while the enclosed propulsion design physically separates the pilot from the high-speed propellers. Urban reflects CoolFly’s broader ambition to rethink what a personal aircraft can look and feel like when it is designed around the individual rather than around conventional aircraft layouts.

Although the three aircraft differ in configuration and performance, they share the same underlying philosophy: making personal flight more intuitive, compact and accessible.

They also share one of CoolFly’s most important technologies — the **NA80 flight control system**.

## NA80: The Technology Behind CoolFly’s Personal Aircraft

For a personal aircraft, getting off the ground is only one part of the engineering challenge. Maintaining stable control, continuously understanding aircraft motion and responding rapidly when a system anomaly occurs are fundamental to safe manned flight.

That is why CoolFly has made flight-control technology a central part of its in-house development strategy.

The **NA80 is CoolFly’s self-developed flight control system built specifically for electric VTOL aircraft**. It uses a dual-redundant flight-control architecture consisting of two independent flight-control units. Each unit integrates **three aviation-grade inertial measurement units (IMUs)**, creating multiple layers of sensing and control redundancy.

During flight, the system continuously monitors the aircraft’s attitude and motion. In the event of an abnormality, the architecture is designed to support **millisecond-level fault detection and seamless system failover**, allowing the redundant flight-control system to respond significantly faster than human reaction time.

![A couple take pictures at CoolFly booth](/assets/img/news/coolfly-brings-its-vision-for-personal-flight-to-ifa-berlin-2026-3.jpg)

Rather than developing a separate control architecture for each aircraft, CoolFly uses NA80 as a common technological foundation across Dream ST, Dream Pro and Urban.

This allows flight-control algorithms, aircraft dynamics, propulsion integration and intelligent flight capabilities to be developed as part of a unified system, an important element of CoolFly’s full-stack approach to personal aircraft development.

For CoolFly, this capability is particularly important as its aircraft evolve from lightweight recreational platforms to higher-performance and more intelligent personal flight systems.

**Dream ST, Dream Pro and Urban have all obtained CE and FCC certifications for applicable product compliance requirements.**
