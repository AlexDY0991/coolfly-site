Most people think of flying during warm, sunny days. But what happens when the temperature drops? Cold weather is usually a big problem for machines with batteries and sensors. Batteries lose power quickly in the frost. Sensors can get confused by the freezing air. However, CoolFly has built a machine that does not fear the winter.

![](/assets/img/news/flying-in-the-cold-how-coolfly-handles-the-ice-1.webp)

## **Staying Warm on the Inside**

The secret to this steady performance is the brain of the vehicle. CoolFly uses a flight controller called the NA80. This is not a standard computer. It uses a special “temperature compensation system”. This system monitors the heat of the internal parts. It makes sure the electronics stay at the right temperature even when the outside air is bitter. Because of this, the flight sensors stay accurate. You can absolutely trust the machine to stay level and stable during your flight.

![](/assets/img/news/flying-in-the-cold-how-coolfly-handles-the-ice-2.webp)

## **Tested on the Snowy Slopes**

Is this just a theory? We can look at where these machines are going to find the answer. CoolFly has already signed deals with eight different ski resorts. These resorts will use the vehicles for flight experiences. Ski resorts are some of the coldest places on earth. If a machine can work there, it can work almost anywhere. This real-world use shows that CoolFly is confident in its technology.

![](/assets/img/news/flying-in-the-cold-how-coolfly-handles-the-ice-3.webp)

## **Easy Control with Heavy Gloves**

There is another practical benefit to the CoolFly design. Most drones or planes require you to move tiny sticks with your fingers. This is very hard to do if you are wearing thick winter gloves.CoolFly uses “gravity-sensing control technology”. You control the flight by moving your body weight while standing. This means your hands do not need to do complex work. You can stay warm in your heavy gear and still fly with total control.

## **A Safe Winter Journey**

Safety is even more important when the weather is harsh. The vehicle uses many backup parts to keep you safe. For example, it has six different navigation sensors and two power systems. If the cold air causes one part to have a small error, the other parts will take over. This “redundancy” is a core part of the design. It gives the pilot peace of mind while they soar over a frozen landscape.

Can we fly in the winter? The answer is yes. Technology is finally catching up to our dreams of year-round flight.
