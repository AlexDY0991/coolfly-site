## **The Power of Coolfly Many Motors**

In the past, most helicopters had one big engine and one big rotor. If that engine stopped, the pilot had to be very skilled to land safely. This is not the case for an electric vtol aircraft. These machines use a system called Distributed Electric Propulsion.

This means the aircraft has many small motors instead of one big one.

Most evtol designs have six, eight, or even sixteen motors. We have observed that this is the best way to handle a motor failure. If one motor stops, the other motors just work a little harder. The “brain” of the aircraft, which is the flight control system, rebalances the power in a millisecond.

![operate a machine](/assets/img/news/coolfly-the-fearless-reality-of-a-unique-tech-2.jpg)

Our team at Coolfly has deep roots in this technology. Our R&D Director has spent more than 13 years working on these control systems. We have tested this logic on 6 different mass-produced models. We know exactly how to make the aircraft stay level even if a motor dies. You might feel a small bump, but the vehicle will stay stable. It will then find the nearest safe spot to land.

## **Coolfly** **Batteries and Backup Plans**

The second big fear is the battery. What if it runs out of juice? This is why we use “redundancy.” Redundancy is a fancy word for having a backup plan for every important part.

An electric vtol aircraft does not have just one battery. It has several separate battery packs. They are not connected in a way where one failure kills the whole system. If one pack fails, the others take over. This gives the pilot enough time to land safely.

![on-board computers](/assets/img/news/coolfly-the-fearless-reality-of-a-unique-tech-3.jpg)

I have seen how energy management has improved over the years. Modern systems monitor every single cell in a battery. They can predict a problem before it happens. At Coolfly, our on-board computers handle these real-time decisions. The vehicle knows exactly how much power it has left. It will never let you start a trip if it does not have enough energy to finish it, plus a big safety margin for emergencies. This is a standard rule in aviation. **[According to the Federal Aviation Administration, “safe flight operations require a minimum fuel reserve to reach an alternate landing site” (FAA 14).](https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/faa-h-8083-25c.pdf)** For us, that “fuel” is electricity.

## **The Role of Glide and Parachutes**

What if the worst happens and all power is lost? This is a very rare event, but engineers still plan for it. Many vtol aircraft are designed to glide. Even without power, the wings or the shape of the body can create lift as the vehicle moves forward. This allows the pilot to steer toward a clear area.

![The Role of Glide and Parachutes](/assets/img/news/coolfly-the-fearless-reality-of-a-unique-tech-4.jpg)

For even more safety, many companies use a Ballistic Recovery System. This is a giant parachute that is shot out of the aircraft with a small rocket. It can carry the weight of the entire vehicle and the passengers down to the ground slowly.

I believe this is the ultimate safety net. It takes away the “fear of falling.” Even if everything stops working, the parachute ensures a soft landing. This technology has already saved hundreds of lives in small private planes. Bringing it to the evtol market is a natural step. It makes the sky a much friendlier place for everyone.
